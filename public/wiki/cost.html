What it costs

Building this twin cost about a dollar. Running it on a real floor costs a few thousand dollars of sensors and about $12 a month on AWS. Omniverse on a rented GPU is the one line that can grow, so it runs only on demand.

Directional, not a quote. Prices are public list prices checked on 2026-10-19 (us-east-1 on demand unless noted). What was tried was limited to what one individual AWS account could access, including its quotas and organisation policies. An enterprise account may see negotiated discounts, savings plans and different quotas, so treat every figure as directional. Taxes, shipping, installation and staff time are not included.

Options

  • Omniverse on a GPU. Marketplace Omniverse workstation on g6e.xlarge (1× L40S): $2.86 an hour, about $124 a month at 10 h a week. A self-managed g6.xlarge (1× L4), as used in the trial, is about $0.80 an hour of compute before licensing: about $35 a month at 10 h a week (about $44 on g5.xlarge), plus a few minutes of start-up per cold job. A stopped instance's disk still bills.
  • Forklift telematics instead of BLE: about $25 to $50 per truck per month, $200 to $400 for 8 trucks.
  • 360° camera to re-capture the building when the layout changes: $400 to $560 once.
  • Containers: ECS on Fargate for jobs, billed per second and scaled to zero; an always-on 0.25 vCPU task is about $7 a month, so the API stays on Lambda.
